Your BMT company gets more canteen breaks, books out suspiciously early and somehow avoids the worst tekan. The explanation will arrive sooner or later: **confirm got white horse**.

A white horse in NS means the son of someone influential. Depending on who is telling the story, that could mean a senior officer, politician, senior civil servant, or wealthy and well-connected person.

The term usually comes out when people think one serviceman, or his entire company, is getting better treatment because of his family. The funny thing is that the SAF really did have a white horse classification before 2000. It was just officially meant to do the opposite of what everyone assumed.

## TLDR

- The SAF used a **white horse classification before 2000**
- It identified sons of influential people
- The Government said it was used to prevent preferential treatment, especially in medical classification and vocation assignments
- The classification was discontinued in 2000
- MINDEF confirmed in 2011 that medical dockets were no longer stamped as white horse
- Today, "white horse" survives as NS slang and folklore
- A relaxed BMT company is not proof that someone inside is a white horse

## The classification

In November 2003, NCMP Steve Chia asked the question everyone wanted answered: did the SAF have a white horse classification, who counted as one and what privileges did they get? Minister of State for Defence Cedric Foo confirmed that the classification had existed before 2000. It identified sons of influential people so their medical classification and vocation assignments could receive extra checks. He said white horses received no special privileges.

For example, if a white horse was assessed as medically unfit, the decision needed an extra review. Fit white horses were supposed to serve in ground units instead of quietly landing a comfortable headquarters job.

This is the strange part of the white horse system: people assumed the label meant "handle with care," while the official explanation was closer to "make sure nobody pulls strings."

The SAF stopped using the classification in 2000. In a 2011 parliamentary exchange about fairness in NS, Defence Minister Ng Eng Hen was asked directly whether medical dockets were still stamped with the white horse marking. His answer was no.

## What is a white horse company?

A **white horse company** is supposedly a BMT company with the son of someone powerful inside. The theory normally appears after recruits compare notes:

- One company gets better food
- They receive unusually frequent canteen breaks
- Their commanders are less interested in tekan
- They book out earlier or book in later
- Training seems more organised, careful or relaxed
- Senior commanders or visitors appear more often

Someone then decides there must be a minister's son, general's son or some other mystery VIP in the company. Nobody knows who he is, but somehow everyone knows he is there.

These stories are common because BMT companies really can have different experiences. Command teams plan and run training differently. Schedules, welfare, discipline and company culture also vary between batches. A more relaxed company does not require a secret explanation.

Usually, nobody can prove the classic "my friend's company had a white horse" story. It is bunk speculation unless someone has actual evidence.

## Does being related to a commander make you a white horse?

Not normally. Having an uncle who was a corporal or a parent who served as a junior commander does not make someone influential across the SAF.

Today, people usually reserve the term for somebody closely related to a very senior officer, politician or another person with real influence. Even then, the family connection does not prove that he received special treatment.

Sometimes the label is just a joke. One popular Reddit post asked whether having an uncle who was a former Lance Corporal made the writer a white horse. The replies promoted him to royalty fairly quickly.

## Do white horses get into OCS?

No, being called a white horse does not automatically send someone to [Officer Cadet School](/officer-cadet-school).

Command school selection considers performance and suitability. Vocation postings also depend on operational and manpower requirements. One story about somebody getting a convenient posting does not prove there is a secret express lane into OCS.

The existence of the old classification does not prove every later claim about preferential treatment.

## Why the term survived

The official classification disappeared, but the idea is almost impossible to kill.

NS places people from very different backgrounds into the same system. Recruits also compare everything: food, welfare, punishments, commanders, book-out timings and postings. When two companies receive noticeably different treatment, "white horse" gives people a simple and entertaining explanation.

It also reflects a serious concern. NS is compulsory, so people care deeply about whether the same rules apply to everyone. MINDEF described equity as a cardinal principle of NS in its 2011 parliamentary reply and said commanders must apply policies with proper reasons and lead by example.

So yes, the white horse was real. No, that does not mean every lepak company is secretly protecting a VIP's son.
