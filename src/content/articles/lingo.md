Army talk is full of abbreviations, Malay commands and words that make no sense before enlistment.

This is the quick dictionary. For insignia and forms of address, see [SAF ranks](/ranks). For parade language, see [marching commands](/commands).

## Common terms people search for

### What does saikang mean?

**Saikang** means an annoying or menial job nobody wants to do. Think moving stores, washing vehicles, raking leaves or doing random jobs for Encik.

### What does POP mean in NS?

**POP** means Passing Out Parade. It marks the end of BMT. You are still an NSF after POP, but you move on to command school or your vocation.

### What does PTP mean in NS?

**PTP** means Physical Training Phase. It is the extra eight weeks before BMT for PES A or B1 enlistees who did not clear the required pre-enlistee IPPT score.

### What do SBO and FBO mean?

**SBO** means Skeletal Battle Order. It normally includes your helmet, Load Bearing System and rifle.

**FBO** means Full Battle Order. It normally adds your field pack to the SBO load.

Some units use terms such as SBO 0 or SBO 1 for different load states. The exact packing list can differ, so follow what your commanders give you.

### What does SOL mean in NS?

**SOL** means Stoppage of Leave. It is a punishment where you cannot leave camp for a set number of days.

### What does coy mean in the army?

**Coy** is short for company. In BMT, a company is split into platoons, and each platoon is split into sections.

### What does ASA mean in NS?

**ASA** means Admin Support Assistant. It is the SAF clerk vocation, covering personnel, records and other office work. See the [logistics vocation guide](/logistics-vocation) for the difference between an ASA and a Supply Assistant.

### What does OOC mean in NS?

**OOC** means Out of Course. You stop your current course, usually because of medical, training or administrative reasons. What happens next depends on the course and why you OOC.

### What does upperstudy mean?

Your **upperstudy** is the person whose job you are taking over. They teach you the role before they ORD or move elsewhere. You become the understudy while learning from them.

## NS and service

- **NS**: National Service. This covers full-time service and the Operationally Ready phase after ORD.
- **SAF**: Singapore Armed Forces.
- **NSF**: Full-time National Serviceman.
- **NSman**: An Operationally Ready National Serviceman who has completed full-time NS.
- **ORD**: Operationally Ready Date. Your last day of full-time NS.
- **ORNS**: Operationally Ready National Service. The reservist phase after ORD.
- **ICT**: In-Camp Training. Your NS unit calls you back for training after ORD.

## BMT and training

- **BMT**: Basic Military Training. The first major training phase for most SAF enlistees.
- **BMTC / BMT(C)**: Basic Military Training Centre. Usually what people mean when talking about Tekong BMT.
- **BTP**: Basic Trainfire Package. Your basic live-firing package during BMT.
- **ATP**: Advanced Trainfire Package. A later live-firing package after BTP.
- **BIC**: Battle Inoculation Course. A short exercise where recruits move through a simulated battlefield.
- **CQB**: Close Quarter Battle. Training for fighting in buildings and built-up areas.
- **FTP**: Field Training Programme. Training exercises conducted outfield.
- **SIT test**: Situational Test. A field exercise used to assess leadership for command school selection. See the [SIT test guide](/sit-test).
- **SCS**: Specialist Cadet School. Graduates normally become Third Sergeants.
- **OCS**: Officer Cadet School. Graduates normally become Second Lieutenants.
- **SOC**: Standard Obstacle Course.
- **IPPT**: Individual Physical Proficiency Test. The SAF fitness test covering push-ups, sit-ups and a 2.4 km run. See the [IPPT guide](/ippt).
- **UIP**: Unit Induction Programme. The introduction and training period after you arrive at a new unit.
- **Outfield**: Training outside camp, normally in field conditions. Expect less sleep, no shower and plenty of equipment.

## Equipment and inspections

- **SBA**: Stand By Area. A bunk or area inspection.
- **LBS**: Load Bearing System. The vest and webbing used to carry equipment.
- **SAR 21**: Singapore Assault Rifle 21. The standard rifle most recruits train with.
- **Force prep**: Force preparation. Checking, laying out and packing equipment before an exercise or outfield.
- **Turnout**: An unplanned fall-in or equipment check, often at an inconvenient time. Move fast and bring what you were told to bring.

## Medical and statuses

- **PES**: Physical Employment Standard. The medical grade used to decide training and vocation suitability. See the [PES guide](/pes).
- **MCS**: Medical Classification System. It replaces PES for pre-enlistees enlisting from October 2027 onwards.
- **MO**: Medical Officer. The doctor you see when reporting sick in camp.
- **RSI**: Report Sick In-camp.
- **RSO**: Report Sick Outside.
- **MC**: Medical certificate. In NS, this normally means you are on Attend C and resting at home.
- **Attend B / LD**: Light duty. You still report for duty but are excused from activities covered by your status.
- **Attend C**: Excused from all duties. Depending on the situation, you may rest at home on MC or remain in the sickbay. See the [Attend C guide](/what-is-attend-c).

## Roles, ranks and discipline

- **SA**: Supply Assistant, commonly called a storeman.
- **TO / TPT OPR**: Transport Operator. An SAF driver.
- **Spec**: Specialist. Usually a sergeant who commands and trains soldiers.
- **Encik**: A common way to address a warrant officer. It literally means "uncle" in Malay, but do not use it casually for every older serviceman.
- **SCT**: Specialist Cadet. Someone training in SCS.
- **3SG**: Third Sergeant. The first specialist rank after graduating from SCS.
- **2LT**: Second Lieutenant. The first commissioned officer rank after graduating from OCS.
- **AWOL**: Absent Without Leave. Being away from duty without permission.
- **DB**: Detention Barracks. The military detention facility.

## Army slang

- **Kenna**: To get subjected to something, usually something bad. Example: "I kenna SOL."
- **Bobo shooter**: Someone who cannot shoot properly.
- **Wayang**: Putting on a show to impress commanders or look busier than you are.
- **Rabak**: Very bad, messy or exhausting. Example: "That outfield was rabak."
- **Chao keng**: Pretending to be sick or injured to avoid work. Do not use it for someone with a real medical issue.
- **Drop / knock it down**: Get into the push-up position.
- **Semula**: Undo the last drill movement and return to the previous position.

## Common phrases

- **Act blur, live longer**: Pretend you do not know what is happening so you do not get picked for extra work.
- **You think, I thought, who confirm?**: What someone says when you give an answer based on an assumption instead of checking.

## Unit structure

Smallest to largest:

- Soldier
- Section: usually 6 to 16 people
- Platoon: usually 2 to 6 sections
- Company: usually 2 to 4 platoons
- Battalion: multiple companies
- Brigade: multiple battalions
- Division: multiple brigades

In BMT, a section is usually around 16 recruits. Four sections form a platoon, and four platoons form a company. Exact numbers change between batches.
