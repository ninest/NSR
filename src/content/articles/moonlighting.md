If you're here to plan your GrabFood delivery empire or launch the next big dropshipping hustle from your bunk, this is not the place for you.

But if you're broke, your family needs help, or you're just curious where the line is, read on. This is what the rules actually are, what happens to people who cross them, and what changes the day you [ORD](/ord-countdown).

## Quick version

- Full-time NSFs are **not allowed to undertake external activities involving remuneration**
- Paid gig work, freelance work, tuition, and actively operating a business can fall within that rule
- If you are unsure whether an activity counts, ask your unit commander before doing it
- If you face financial hardship, approach your commanders for financial assistance
- After ORD, the full-time restriction ends, but NSmen must still follow the rules that apply during call-ups and service

## Why it's banned

The published CMPB rule is direct: NSFs must be committed to their NS responsibilities and are not allowed to undertake external activities involving remuneration.

If you are struggling financially, CMPB specifically advises approaching your unit commanders to apply for financial assistance and explore other help.

## What counts as moonlighting

More than people assume:

- Paid delivery or driving work
- Paid event or banquet shifts
- Tuition and freelance work
- Paid work performed through an online platform
- Actively operating a business for remuneration

Some situations are less obvious, such as receiving occasional income from something created before enlistment or holding a business registration without doing active work. Do not invent your own exception. Describe the arrangement to your unit and ask for an official answer before continuing.

## What happens if the rule is breached

A suspected breach can be investigated and handled through the applicable service disciplinary process. The facts, offence, procedure, and outcome depend on the individual case.

Do not use a Reddit case to estimate your likely fine or assume that another person's investigation describes what will happen to you. If you are under investigation, follow the official instructions you receive and ask about the legal or defending-officer assistance available to you.

This site also has no reliable basis to make claims about routine bank, phone, GPS, or camp-network monitoring, so it does not speculate about them.

## After ORD

Once you are lawfully released from full-time NS, the NSF restriction on remunerative external activities no longer applies to your ordinary civilian employment.

As an NSman, you remain subject to ORNS obligations and to military law in the circumstances set out by the SAF Act, including while ordered to report, in uniform, or performing service. Your civilian work must not stop you from meeting those obligations.

If you sign on as a regular, the employment and conflict-of-interest rules for regular service apply.

## If your situation is unusual

Do not assume that financial hardship, a pre-existing business, family responsibilities, or a small amount of income creates an automatic exception. Raise the situation with your unit commander and follow the written direction you receive.
