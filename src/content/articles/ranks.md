The SAF uses the same rank insignia and titles across the Army, Navy, and Air Force since 1982. The tables below use Army rank titles. Navy equivalents are noted where they differ (generals and officer cadets). The Air Force uses identical titles to the Army.

*For current monthly allowance figures by rank and vocation, see [NSF pay and allowance](/nsf-allowance).*

### Sir, Sergeant, or Encik?
This is a question most BMT recruits have:
- arrows pointing down: sergeant (see [3SG](#specialists))
- horizontal lines: sir (see [2LT](#junior-officers))
- arrows pointing up: encik (see [3WO](#warrant-officers))

This isn't a "one rule fits all", but in BMT the ranks you usually see are 3SG/2SG (sergeant), WOs (encik), 2LT/CPT (sir).

SAFVC volunteers are not paid a monthly salary. They receive a per-day SV Allowance during call-ups, pro-rated from the NSF rank allowance. If call-up causes civilian income loss, employed volunteers can claim Make-Up Pay (the difference). Uniform, lodging, food, and medical are provided during training. There is no eMart, IPPT incentive, or tax relief. "Volunteer" here means the scheme is not mandatory, not that it's unpaid.

## Vocation allowance

| Tier | Examples | Allowance (S$) |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Service / technical | clerk, storeman, technician | 75 |
| General combat | most combat vocations | 225 |
| Higher-risk combat | infantry, armour, guards, aircrew, combat medics in MRF/SCDF ambulances, seagoing | 300 |
| Specialised high-risk | commando, naval diver, CBRD, EOD | 500 |

Example: A general combat PTE gets $790 + $150 = $940. The $75 service minimum is already in the $790, so combat adds the $150 difference.
